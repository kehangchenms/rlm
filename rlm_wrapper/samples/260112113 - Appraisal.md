## Definition of Market Value (USPAP Definition)

The most probable price which a property should bring in a competitive and open market under all conditions, requisite to a fair sale, the buyer and seller each acting prudently, knowledgeably and assuming the price is not affected by undue stimulus. Implicit in this definition is the consummation of a sale as of a specific date and the passing of title from seller to buyer under conditions whereby:

1. Buyer and seller are typically motivated;
2. Both parties are well informed or well advised, and each acting in what they consider their own best interests;
3. A reasonable time is allowed for exposure in the open market;
4. Payment is made in terms of cash in United States dollars or in terms of financial arrangements comparable thereto; and
5. The price represents the normal consideration for the property sold unaffected by special or creative financing or sales concessions granted by anyone associated with the sale.1

# REGIONAL ANALYSIS

At 525.8 square miles, Blair County is one of the smallest counties in the state and has a population density of 241.7 persons per square mile. Transportation industries originally developed in Blair County, and it remains well located with respect to major eastern markets. Tussey Mountain borders it on the east and the Allegheny Mountains border to the west.

![img-1.jpeg](img-1.jpeg)

The development of Blair County may be traced in terms of transportation from the original Indian Trails, the Pennsylvania Canal, the Portage Railroad and the nearby Pennsylvania Turnpike. More recently, limited-access highways constructed in the county have fueled additional development. The creation of the county's largest city, Altoona, was largely due to railroads. Altoona is home of the Pennsylvania Railroad's largest construction and repair facilities.

Altoona was incorporated as a borough in 1846 and chartered as a city in 1868. It is located 1,171 feet above sea level near the source of the Juniata River and is surrounded by magnificent scenery, including the world famous "Horseshoe Curve". South of Altoona are Historic Hollidaysburg, the county seat, and the charming rural Boroughs of Martinsburg, Roaring Spring, and Williamsburg, all serving the Morrison's Cove area of the county. To the north of Altoona are the growing residential Boroughs of Bellwood and Tyrone, adjacent to the emerging I-99 (formerly US Route 220) corridor.

The history of Blair County can be traced through its service as a transportation hub and as the center of the emerging Route 220 growth corridor. U.S. Route 220, the backbone of the corridor, is a new limited access highway from its intersection with the Pennsylvania Turnpike in Bedford north to Bald Eagle, which is north of Tyrone. It has been upgraded between Bald Eagle to State College as well as its connection to I-80 in Centre County. U.S. Route 22 is limited access west to its connection with U.S. Route 219 to Johnstown.

With its Eastern Market Place proximity, wage rates significantly below the national average, and well-developed transportation infrastructure, Blair County may well be an important factor in mixing point, distribution center, and warehousing plans. International cargo container availability is through the Port of Baltimore, just three hours away. A Blair County location affords one-day service to five of the ten major U.S. Markets, a major portion of the Canadian population, and almost half of the Nation's manufacturing trade and service industries.

The construction of new and improved highways and connectors has dramatically improved access to Blair County and created a customer base of 900,000 potential shoppers within a 50 to 80 mile radius.

Concurrent with the growth in retailing has been the creation in Blair County of a "balanced sector" economy. Manufacturing, services, and now retail each contribute approximately 26% of the jobs to the Blair County economy. This unusual balance among sectors of the economy provides stability against sudden economic shifts and has resulted in both a diverse and more sophisticated type of market area.

There are over 700 retail establishments in Blair County, which employ over 11,000 people. Many of these are centrally located near the City of Altoona and the Borough of Hollidaysburg.

# HIGHEST AND BEST USE

That reasonable and probable use that will support the highest present value, as defined as of the effective date of the appraisal.

Alternatively, that use, from among reasonable probable and legal alternative uses, found to be physically possible, appropriately supported, financially feasible, and which results in the highest land value.

The definition immediately above applies specifically to the highest and best use of land. It is to be recognized that in cases where a site has existing improvements on it, the highest and best use may vary well be determined to be different from the existing use. The existing use will continue, however, unless and until land value in its highest and best use exceeds the total value of the property in its exiting use.

Implied within these definitions is recognition of the contribution of that specific use to community environment or to community development goals in addition to wealth maximization of individual property owners.

Also implied is that the determination of highest and best use results from the appraiser’s judgment and analytical skill, i.e., that the use determined from analysis represents an opinion, not a fact to be found. In appraisal practice, the concept of highest and best use represents the premises upon which value is based. In the context of investment value an alternative term would be most profitable use.

# THE VALUATION PROCESS

There are typically three accepted approaches to valuation. These approaches are known as the cost approach, the sales comparison approach, and the income approach. According to generally-accepted appraisal theory, each approach provides a semi-independent method of estimating the value of real property.

The cost approach is based on the principle of substitution. This principle suggests that a prudent investor would pay no more for a property than the cost to acquire the site and construct an improvement of equal desirability and utility. The cost approach involves five steps in arriving at an estimate of value. The first step is to estimate the value of the land as if vacant and available for development to its highest and best use.

The next step is to estimate the reproduction or replacement cost of the improvements as of the date of this appraisal. The third step is to estimate any accrued depreciation for physical deterioration, functional obsolescence and external obsolescence. The next step is to subtract the accrued depreciation from the reproduction or replacement cost new to arrive at the depreciated cost new of the improvements. And finally, the last step is to add the depreciated value of the improvements to the estimated land value, resulting in an estimated market value by the cost approach. The cost approach is particularly useful and important when appraising special-purpose properties. The cost approach is developed in this appraisal report since one of the buildings is relatively newer and the fact that the development of this approach was requested by the client/lender.

The sales comparison approach, also referred to as the market approach, compares recent sales of similar properties to the subject property. Each sale is adjusted to reflect any dissimilarities as compared to the subject property. The sales comparison approach is generally pertinent in the valuation of most real estate since it interprets the actual behavior of buyers and sellers in the marketplace. This is particularly true when ample and adequate comparable sales can be found within the market area for substitute properties.

For this assignment, the appraiser has searched for nearby and similar warehouse/garage/service buildings. The most recent and similar sales in the immediate and nearby towns have been presented. Although there are a limited number of these types of sales, it was still possible to accurately and reliably prepare the sales comparison method. Consequently, the sales comparison (market) approach is presented in this appraisal report.

The income approach measures a property's income stream and is very important in the valuation of income-producing properties, as they are typically purchased for investment purposes. This approach is comprised of several related techniques and procedures that are designed to produce a convincing and reliable estimate of value. In any case, all income approach techniques attempt to accurately forecast future benefits and calculate the present value of the benefits to a typical investor.

There are insufficient rent comparables for similar properties of similar location, size, age, and condition. In addition, capitalization rates are also difficult to ascertain for these types of buildings. As a result, the income approach to value will not be developed.

# MARKET APPROACH ANALYSIS

The attached comparable improved sales provide insight on the market value of the subject property. A search for similar garage and warehouse buildings was performed in the immediate and surrounding areas of the subject. Some nearby sales existed, but a slightly expanded geographic search was performed to properly bracket all of the subject’s features. The range of the attached comparable sales’ unadjusted sale prices are between $42.29 per square foot to $52.08 per square foot.

Comparable #1 was sold just over five years ago. It was located across from the Giant Eagle store in Roaring Spring. It was attached to bracket the subject’s building size. It had a slightly superior location and quality of construction. It suffered from an inferior condition. This was an older and inferior comparable and was adjusted upward to better compare to the subject.

Sale #2 is a building facility located in the town of Newry. It was located behind some residential dwellings along a back access road so it suffered from an inferior location. Due to the economies of scale principle, it required an adjustment for its smaller building size. Although this sale only had two differences with the subject, these differences offset each other so there was no net adjustment in its price per square foot. Significant weight was given to this sale in the subject’s final value determination.

Improved Sale #3 is another fairly similar sale. It was a garage building sale that occurred in 2024. It had an inferior location (along a hillside and not along a main thoroughfare). This sale had a slightly superior condition and a smaller total building area. This was a slightly superior sale overall and just needed a modest downward net adjustment in its price per square foot. Significant weight was also placed on this sale.

Considering all of the comparable sales, more weight is placed on the sales requiring less net adjustments, namely Comps #2 and #3. Less weight was given to the comparable sale with the highest percentage of net adjustments, namely Comp #1. As a result of the qualitative adjustments discussed earlier on within this market approach analysis section and the quantitative adjustments found in the previous sales grid (located on page 51), the subject’s indicated real estate only market value per square foot is $51.50. # INCOME APPROACH TO AS-IS VALUE

As defined by the American Institute of Real Estate Appraiser, the Income Approach to Value is defined as follows.

That procedure in appraisal analysis that converts anticipated benefits (dollar income or amenities) to be derived from the ownership of property into a value estimate. The income approach is widely applied in appraising income-producing properties. Anticipated future income and/or reversions are discounted to a present worth figure if the future income and values are expected to have an inconsistent pattern. If the future income and value are expected to experience normal growth, then the direct capitalization method is used to convert stabilized earnings into an estimate of market value.

This approach to value has not been fully developed due to the fact that subject property is entirely owner occupied. Most of these types of building are owner occupied making available market rents and appropriate capitalization rates difficult to obtain. Due to the fact that this necessary information is so limited, this approach will not be developed.
